Too Many Hats

April 23, 2026

At this point of my life, my head fills many hats.

On the weekdays, in the morning I am a student of mathematics. I wake up, eat my breakfast, stare at my homework (and if I'm lucky, solve a problem or two.) Then, I drive to school, where I attend my classes and discuss problems with friends.

In the afternoon, I am a climbing coach. I switch gears from being a learner to being a teacher. Once I finish coaching, I might climb myself, once again becoming a student, listening to what the wall might teach me. On other days, I go to be a math tutor, becoming a teacher again.

Sometimes, the teacher, tutor, coach, and climber in me feel like one cohesive person, and sometimes they feel like four different people.

Sometimes, it is overwhelming to keep track of all the hats I must wear at specific times during the week. Often, the 168 hours of the week don't feel like enough to do everything I would like to do. I think to myself, if I had just one extra hour per day, I would feel less tired. I would have the mental bandwidth to wear all of my hats just a bit better. Of course, it is my own fault - I chose to take on all of these responsibilities.

Other times, I feel guilty for being tired. I get to spend my entire week doing things that I love! How many people have the privilage to wear four different hats that they genuinely want to be wearing? How can I complain about having no time, when all of my time is used for learning things I want to learn, teaching things I want to teach, mentoring people I care about?

Recently, I've been trying to convince myself that these two feelings can coexist with each other. I should allow myself to feel anxious about my lack of free time, sad about the things I am missing because of homework, while also being thankful for all the things I do get to do, and happy about the time I spend doing the things I care about. I'm doing my best to remind myself that emotions aren't a linear scale of good to bad, but a higher-dimensional space of experiences in which seemingly contradictory feelings can exist on orthogonal planes.
