Effort Is Vulnerable

October 31, 2026

As both a coach and an athlete, recently I've been wondering about what makes it so hard to give full effort. I love climbing and want to improve, so why do I feel a reluctance to commit to a hard training plan? The kids that I coach say over and over again that they want to improve, so why do they try to slack on their drills and avoid training for their goals?

Of course, there's the obvious answer that humans are lazy and short-sighted and try to take the path of least resistance, even if it means decreased results in the long run. But for me, at least, when I reflect on this, I think it goes deeper. In my mind, in addition to just requiring willpower, giving 100% at something also requires humility, vulnerability, and responsibility - all of which are somewhat uncomfortable.

Effort requires humility: when you decide to train hard at something, you are admitting that you aren't as good as you would like to be at this thing. You are making a public statement that you are not good enough, and need to improve.

Effort requires vulnerability: whenever you start working hard at something, you have to face the questions: what if it doesn't work out? What if I don't place at this competition? If people see me working this hard, and I go and produce no results, what will they think? Future failures can no longer be blamed on "I haven't really been practicing," or "I could have trained more."

Effort requires responsibility: if you decide to place effort into improving some specific strength, it is a declaration that you can exercise a degree of control over that strength. It's no longer just decided by your genetics, or the way your brain is wired, or your upbringing. You eliminate any excuses you had before about why you lack this strength.

These are the deeper reasons that make it hard for me to give 100% effort on anything, whether that be coaching, school, climbing, or this website project. But, I hope that writing these thoughts down will help me give quality effort throughout this next year of training and study and competitions and exams.

While the occasional lack of effort among climbers originally inspired me to write this, recently there have been a couple of climbers that I coach who I really admire for their effort. They make me want to start training hard again, too!

Just so I have it in writing: this season, my goal is to be able to look back at the end of it, and feel like I put my best effort into training for Collegiate Nationals, with an particular emphasis on the Lead discipline.
