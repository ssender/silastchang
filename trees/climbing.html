Climbing, in Its Purest Form

May 30, 2026

What is the purest form of climbing?

A boulderer might argue that the way bouldering strips down climbing to its bare essentials, just hands and feet on the rock, pushing the human limit, is the purest form of climbing. A trad climber might argue that, traditional climbing is pure in the sense that it is closest to the sport's roots in mountaineering. A speed climber might argue that... actually I'm not sure what a speed climber would argue.

I'm not really here to argue one way or another. Rather, I beleive that the process of searching for the answer to this question itself is the essence of climbing. Climbing, in its purest form, is some people in front of a rock, trying to find the sickest way to get up the rock.

Of course, there are a couple of words in this claim that should be defined, the first of which is rock. Here, I am using the word in the loosest possible sense - "rock" refers to some object which may be more or less dificult to climb. It could be a literal rock, a cliff, an artificial wall, a building...

The second term which must be defined is sickest . This definition is where people tend to disagree most. An outdoor boulderer might have one definition of the word, approximately "hardest, most technical, and most aesthetically pleasing." Competitions declare the definition of sickest to "whatever gets you the most points." Speed climbers have redefined to word to mean "fastest way up this one particular wall that hasn't been reset for like 15 years." I've met people obsessed with boards, who would probably define the word differently. I've never understood the board climbing obsession, so I won't attempt to describe this type of person's definition of "sick."

In reality, I think that everyone has their own definition of what it means for a climb to be "sick." Regardless of how one defines it, "sickness" is an aesthetic property, impossible to pin down quantitatively. But every climber pursues their definition of "sickness," some to extreme degrees. This common will to train, travel, and work as a team to climb in the way that we see as most "beautiful" or "satisfying", is what makes me believe this is the essence of our sport.

Of course, there's an argument that goes something like this: the purest form of climbing is the one which is most natural, and the least contrived. So, the person who enjoys "pure" climbing should have the least "contrived" definition of "sick." But to that I respond, is not all of modern climbing contrived? We get to the top of whatever project we had, and then we return to the ground, back where we started.

Anyway, this is why I think routesetting is the coolest job. That's right, you thought this essay was a thougtful reflection on the nature of climbing as a pasttime when in reality it was ROUTESETTER PROPAGANDA! As a routesetter, you get paid to come up with sick ways to get up a rock. In other words, it's your daily job to interact with what I believe is climbing in its purest form.
